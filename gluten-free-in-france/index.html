Gluten free in France

Sans gluten on a French label means 20 mg of gluten per kilogram or less. The épi de blé barré is the licensed Crossed Grain symbol, and the ingredient list names the cereal: blé, orge, seigle, avoine, épeautre. Save this for your next trip ✈️

What sans gluten means

France applies Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 828/2014. Sans gluten may be used when the food, as sold, contains no more than 20 mg of gluten per kilogram (20 ppm).

Très faible teneur en gluten means very low gluten: no more than 100 mg/kg, and only for food that contains wheat, rye, barley, oats or their crosses specially processed to reduce gluten. The statement for the 20 mg/kg standard is sans gluten.

Oats in a sans gluten food have to be specially produced to avoid wheat, rye and barley, and the oats themselves stay at or under 20 mg/kg. The word avoine is still emphasised in the ingredients.

A restaurant or bakery that describes a dish as sans gluten is using the same statement, so the 20 mg/kg rule applies there too.

Sans gluten means 20 mg/kg or less. Très faible teneur en gluten is the separate 100 mg/kg statement.

The épi de blé barré

The crossed ear of wheat is the AOECS Crossed Grain trademark. In France, AFDIAG licenses it. A licensed product meets the AOECS standard of 20 mg/kg or less, with production checks. The symbol carries a licence code, and French codes start with FR. Products that contain oats show the word OATS with the symbol.

Sans gluten is the legal statement and stands on its own. The symbol means AFDIAG licensed that product on top of the statement.

Words on the label

Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011 requires the cereal to be named and emphasised, usually in bold. Look for the grain, not only the word gluten.

Ingredients that often carry gluten

  • Blé, épeautre, petit épeautre and kamut. Épeautre is spelt, a wheat, and ordinary épeautre bread is a wheat food.
  • Orge and malt, including malt in breakfast cereals and drinks.
  • Seigle in bread and flour.
  • Avoine, unless the pack says sans gluten.
  • Amidon de blé on a food that is not sans gluten. A sans gluten food may list wheat starch when the finished food stays at or under 20 mg/kg.
  • Sauce soja brewed with wheat.
  • Chapelure, panure, and a roux or any sauce bound with farine.
  • Bière, unless it is labeled sans gluten.
  • Seitan, which is wheat gluten.

Eating out

Bread arrives with the meal. Sauces often start with a roux. Ask about the flour, the soy sauce and the breadcrumbs, and ask whether the gluten-free dish is fried or plated away from the wheat one. Quiche pastry, breaded cutlets and ordinary beer are wheat or barley unless a sans gluten version is offered.

Questions people ask

What does sans gluten mean in France?

Under EU Regulation 828/2014, sans gluten means the food as sold contains no more than 20 mg of gluten per kilogram. A dish described as sans gluten uses the same statement.

What does "peut contenir des traces de gluten" mean?

It is a voluntary precaution about possible cross-contact with gluten. Cereals that are ingredients are named in the ingredient list, usually in bold: blé, orge, seigle, avoine or épeautre.

Is épeautre gluten free?

Épeautre is spelt, a species of wheat, and it contains gluten. Petit épeautre is einkorn, also wheat. A food made from them is sans gluten when the pack carries that statement and meets the 20 mg/kg rule.

What is the épi de blé barré?

It is the Crossed Grain symbol, licensed in France by AFDIAG, for products at or under 20 mg/kg. The licence code starts with FR. Sans gluten is the legal statement and also appears without the symbol.
