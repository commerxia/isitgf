Gluten free in Australia

Gluten free in Australia is stricter than the 20 ppm rule used in the US, Canada, the UK and the EU. The claim means no detectable gluten, and oats cannot carry it. Save this for your next trip ✈️

Updated 18 November 2026

Gluten free means no detectable gluten

Food Standards Australia New Zealand sets the claim in Schedule 4 of the Food Standards Code. A gluten free food has to meet all three conditions: no detectable gluten, no oats or oat products, and no cereals containing gluten that have been malted, or products of those cereals.

The code does not give this claim a 20 ppm allowance. Laboratories testing for it commonly detect gluten at about 3 to 5 ppm. The legal words remain no detectable gluten. Coeliac Australia describes this as stricter than the rules in the United States, the United Kingdom and the European Union.

A product sold in Australia with a gluten free claim, including an imported product, has to meet the Australian rule. Food you packed in the US or Europe was judged against a 20 ppm gluten-free mark, which is a different standard from the one on Australian shelves.

Gluten free here means no detectable gluten, no oats, and no malted gluten-containing cereals.

Phrases such as gluten friendly or no added gluten are not a stand-in for the gluten free claim. The claim to look for is gluten free.

Low gluten, and why oats are labeled differently

Low gluten is a separate claim in the same schedule. The food contains no more than 20 mg of gluten per 100 g, which is 200 ppm. Coeliac Australia says a low gluten claim is not suitable for people with coeliac disease.

Oats cannot be labeled gluten free in Australia or New Zealand, including oats grown and handled to limit wheat, rye and barley. Some packs say wheat free, pure, uncontaminated or low gluten. Those phrases are not a gluten free claim. Coeliac Australia suggests talking with your medical team before you add oats, and checking any may-contain line for wheat, rye and barley.

Words, precautions, and the endorsement logo

Wheat, barley, rye and oats are declared when they are ingredients, in the ingredient list and in the allergen summary.

May contain and may be present are voluntary. Coeliac Australia's guidance to businesses says a gluten free claim should stand on its own, without a paired line such as may contain gluten or may be present: gluten.

Coeliac Australia licenses an endorsement logo, a crossed grain, for products reviewed under its endorsement programme. The words gluten free remain the legal claim. The logo is an extra review by Coeliac Australia.

Ingredients that often carry gluten

  • Wheat varieties: spelt, durum, semolina, couscous and bulgur.
  • Malt and malt extract from barley. Malted gluten-containing cereals are outside a gluten free claim.
  • Regular oats, and foods made with them. They cannot carry a gluten free claim in Australia.
  • Soy sauce brewed with wheat.
  • Beer made from barley or wheat, unless a gluten free claim that meets the Australian rule is on the label.
  • Breadcrumbs, batter, pastry, meat pies and sausage rolls, unless a gluten free version is offered.
  • Stock, sauces and spice mixes that use wheat flour. The declared allergen name is the check.

Eating out

A gluten free claim on a menu follows the same no-detectable-gluten standard. Ask about the fryer, the sauce and any marinade. Battered fish, meat pies and sausage rolls are wheat dishes unless a gluten free version is stated.

Questions people ask

Is Australian gluten free the same as 20 ppm?

The Food Standards Code requires no detectable gluten, plus no oats and no malted gluten-containing cereals. It does not set a 20 ppm allowance. Tests used for the claim commonly detect gluten at about 3 to 5 ppm, which is stricter than the 20 ppm rule in the US, Canada, the UK and the EU.

Can oats be gluten free in Australia?

Oats and oat products cannot be labeled gluten free in Australia, including specially produced oats. Wheat free, pure or uncontaminated on an oat pack is not that claim. Coeliac Australia suggests deciding about oats with your clinician.

What does low gluten mean?

Low gluten means no more than 20 mg of gluten per 100 g of food, which is 200 ppm. It is a different claim from gluten free. Coeliac Australia says it is not suitable for people with coeliac disease.

What is the crossed grain logo in Australia?

Coeliac Australia licenses an endorsement logo, a crossed grain, for products it has reviewed. The legal claim on the pack is still the words gluten free, under the no-detectable-gluten rule.
