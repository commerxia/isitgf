Gluten free in Canada

On a Canadian label, gluten-free is a regulated claim, and the gluten sources have names: wheat, barley, rye, oats and triticale. Packs are bilingual, so the French line matters as much as the English one. Save this for your next trip ✈️

Updated 18 November 2026

How a gluten-free claim works

Section B.24.018 of the Food and Drug Regulations prohibits selling or labeling a food as gluten-free when it contains gluten protein from wheat (including kamut and spelt), barley, rye, oats, triticale, or a hybrid of those grains.

The regulation itself names the grains rather than a number. Health Canada considers that gluten from cross-contamination at or below 20 ppm meets the health intent of that rule. The Canadian Food Inspection Agency can take enforcement action when a food labeled gluten-free contains more than 20 ppm of gluten. Companies are expected to keep cross-contamination as low as reasonably achievable.

A gluten source that is an ingredient has to be named, in the ingredient list or in a Contains statement, including when the amount is small. The name is the grain.

Look for the word gluten-free. Gluten from cross-contamination on that food is held to 20 ppm or less, and the recipe's gluten sources are named.

Canada does not use a low-gluten or reduced-gluten claim for these foods. Gluten-free is the claim to look for.

Wheat-free and gluten-free answer different questions. Wheat-free addresses wheat. A gluten-free claim also covers barley, rye, regular oats and triticale.

Oats have their own rule

Regular oats stay on the gluten-source list, because they commonly pick up wheat, rye and barley. Health Canada allows a gluten-free claim on specially produced oats, and on foods made with them, when those oats meet the gluten-free oats criteria and the label identifies them as gluten-free oats. The contamination limit in those criteria is 20 ppm from wheat, rye and barley.

On the pack, look for gluten-free oats, or avoine sans gluten. Ordinary "oats" or "avoine" without that claim stay a gluten source.

Whether you include oats is a decision to make with your clinician. The labeling rule is what lets you tell the two kinds of oats apart.

English and French words

Prepackaged food in Canada is labeled in English and French. Read either language. The grain names match.

Ingredients that often carry gluten

  • Malt and malt extract, usually barley. In French, malt.
  • Soy sauce, sauce soya. Many are brewed with wheat.
  • Beer and bière made from barley or wheat, unless the label says gluten-free or sans gluten.
  • Breadcrumbs, chapelure, batter and flour-thickened gravy. Poutine gravy is a flour question worth asking.
  • Seasoning mixes and bouillon bases that use wheat flour. The label names the grain when it is an ingredient.
  • Spelt, kamut, durum, semolina, couscous and bulgur, which are wheat.

Eating out

Ask whether the dish is gluten-free, and ask what thickens the sauce. Flour, soy sauce and a shared fryer are the usual follow-ups.

Questions people ask

Is the Canadian gluten-free limit 20 ppm?

The Food and Drug Regulations ban a gluten-free claim when the food contains gluten protein from wheat, barley, rye, oats, triticale or their hybrids. Health Canada and the CFIA apply 20 ppm to gluten that gets in by cross-contamination: more than 20 ppm can bring enforcement, and companies are expected to stay as far under that as they reasonably can.

Can oats be gluten-free in Canada?

Regular oats are a gluten source and stay off a gluten-free claim. Specially produced oats can be labeled gluten-free oats when they meet Health Canada's criteria, including 20 ppm or less from wheat, rye and barley. The label has to identify them as gluten-free oats.

Which French words should I read?

Sans gluten is the claim. The grains are blé, orge, seigle, avoine and triticale. Peut contenir introduces a voluntary cross-contact precaution. Sans blé means wheat-free, which is a different claim.

Do I only need to look for wheat?

Wheat is one gluten source. The label also has to name barley, rye, oats and triticale when they are ingredients. A wheat-free food can still contain those.
