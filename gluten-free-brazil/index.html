Gluten free in Brazil?
You're gonna LOVE it 🇧🇷🧀

Brazil runs on cassava, and every packaged food has to say whether it contains gluten. Here's what to look for, what to ask about, and a card in Portuguese to show the kitchen. Save this for your next trip ✈️

Cassava and corn favourites to look for

  • Pão de queijo: cheese bread made with polvilho (cassava starch). Ask if it's only polvilho, since some bakeries add wheat flour.
  • Tapioca: a crepe made from cassava starch, filled with cheese, coconut or meat.
  • Feijoada: black bean and pork stew with rice, collard greens and orange.
  • Churrasco: grilled meat at a churrascaria. Ask about marinades and sauces.
  • Pamonha and curau: sweet corn treats, often sold at street stalls.
  • Brigadeiro: chocolate and condensed-milk sweets. Check the sprinkles.

The label law is your friend

Since 2003, Brazilian law (Lei 10.674) requires every packaged food to say "CONTÉM GLÚTEN" or "NÃO CONTÉM GLÚTEN". Look for it near the ingredients at the supermercado.

The phrase tells you about the recipe, not about shared factories. If cross-contact matters to you, read the allergen line too ("pode conter...").

Worth asking about

  • Coxinha, pastel, esfiha and kibe: the dough or filling is usually wheat.
  • Farofa: normally toasted cassava flour, but some versions add breadcrumbs.
  • Açaí bowls: the granola on top usually has oats or wheat. Ask for it without.
  • Shared fryers: fries and mandioca frita may share oil with breaded snacks.
  • Beer (cerveja): made from barley.

Recipes vary by cook and region, so ask every time and check labels. This guide helps you plan; it doesn't replace your own judgement or medical advice.

The restaurant card (Portuguese)

🍴 Sem glúten

Olá! Só para avisar que como sem glúten e não posso consumir glúten.

Nada com trigo, cevada, centeio, aveia (exceto a certificada) nem panados ou molhos com isso.

Podia avisar a cozinha e pedir que, ao preparar a minha comida, evitem usar os mesmos utensílios que usam para comidas com glúten?
