Gluten free in Japan?
You're gonna LOVE it 🇯🇵🍙

Rice is everywhere in Japan, but soy sauce (made with wheat) hides in a lot of dishes. Here's what to look for, what to ask, and a card in Japanese to show the kitchen. Save this for your next trip ✈️

Bring tamari 🍶

Regular Japanese soy sauce (shoyu) is brewed with wheat. Pack a small bottle of gluten free tamari, or ask for wheat-free soy sauce (小麦不使用の醤油). Many supermarkets sell it.

Rice-based favourites to look for

  • Sashimi and nigiri, with your own tamari instead of the soy sauce on the table.
  • Yakitori "shio" (salt) rather than "tare" (a soy-based glaze).
  • Onigiri (rice balls): plain salted ones or salmon are a good start. Check the label for soy sauce.
  • Plain rice, grilled fish (yakizakana) and tamagoyaki: ask whether soy sauce or mirin blends go into them.
  • Mochi and daifuku: made from glutinous rice. Despite the name, glutinous rice contains no gluten; check the filling and coating.

Worth asking about

  • Soy-based sauces: teriyaki, ponzu, tare and many dressings contain shoyu.
  • Miso: mugi miso is made with barley. Rice miso is usually wheat free, but ask.
  • Soba: most soba noodles mix buckwheat with wheat. Even 100% buckwheat soba (juwari) is often cooked in shared water.
  • Tempura, udon, ramen, katsu and gyoza: wheat-based. Skip them.

Recipes vary by cook and region, so ask every time and check labels. This guide helps you plan; it doesn't replace your own judgement or medical advice.

Useful phrases

小麦が食べられません。(Komugi ga taberaremasen.)I can't eat wheat.
醤油に小麦が入っていますか？(Shōyu ni komugi ga haitte imasu ka?)Does the soy sauce contain wheat?
醤油なしでお願いします。(Shōyu nashi de onegai shimasu.)Without soy sauce, please.
塩でお願いします。(Shio de onegai shimasu.)With salt (not tare), please.

The restaurant card (Japanese)

🍴 グルテンフリー

こんにちは！グルテンフリーで、グルテンは食べられないことをお伝えしておきますね。

小麦、大麦、ライ麦、オーツ麦（認証されたもの以外）、それらを使った衣やソースは食べられません。
