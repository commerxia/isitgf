Gluten free in the United Kingdom

Gluten free in the UK means 20 mg of gluten per kilogram or less, on a pack and on a plate. The Crossed Grain symbol is a separate licence on top of those words. Save this for your next trip ✈️

Updated 18 November 2026

Gluten free means 20 mg/kg

The term gluten free is covered by assimilated Regulation (EU) No 828/2014. The food, as sold, contains no more than 20 mg of gluten per kilogram. That is 20 ppm. The same rule covers food in restaurants and catering when those words are used.

Very low gluten is a second, separate statement. It means no more than 100 mg/kg, and it applies to food made with wheat, rye, barley, oats or their crosses that have been specially processed to reduce gluten. When you want the 20 mg/kg standard, the words to find are gluten free.

Oats in a gluten free food have to be specially produced to avoid contamination from wheat, rye and barley, and those oats stay at or under 20 mg/kg. The word oats is still emphasised in the ingredients.

Gluten free: 20 mg/kg or less, on the pack and on a dish that uses those words.

Cereals containing gluten are emphasised in the ingredient list, usually in bold: wheat, rye, barley, oats, spelt and khorasan wheat (often sold as kamut), plus their hybrids. You will usually see the cereal name. The word gluten itself is often absent from the ingredients.

Food that is packed on the premises for direct sale, such as a packaged sandwich made in the shop, needs an ingredient label with those cereals emphasised. In England this is known as Natasha's Law. Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland have equivalent rules.

The Crossed Grain symbol

The Crossed Grain is a registered trademark of the Association of European Coeliac Societies (AOECS). Coeliac UK licenses it for products that meet the AOECS standard of 20 mg/kg or less, with checks on how the food is made. The symbol is printed with a licence code, and UK codes start with UK. When the product contains oats, the word OATS is shown with the symbol.

The words gluten free are the legal statement. Many gluten free foods use the words and do not carry the symbol. The symbol tells you a coeliac society licensed that product.

Food Standards Agency guidance asks businesses to use gluten free when they control the food to 20 mg/kg, and to say clearly when a "no gluten-containing ingredients" list was not prepared to that standard. If a menu uses the shorter NGCI wording, ask which one they mean.

Ingredients that often carry gluten

  • Wheat varieties: spelt, kamut, durum, semolina, couscous and bulgur.
  • Barley malt, malt extract and barley malt vinegar. Malt vinegar on a British label is barley unless the label names another source.
  • Soy sauce. Most traditional soy sauce is brewed with wheat.
  • Beer, ale and lager, unless the drink is labeled gluten free.
  • Flour-thickened gravy, stuffing, Yorkshire pudding, breadcrumbs and batter, including beer batter.
  • Wheat starch in a food that is not labeled gluten free. A gluten free food may list wheat starch when the starch was processed to reduce gluten and the finished food stays at or under 20 mg/kg.
  • Stock cubes and spice mixes that use wheat flour. The bold cereal name is the check.

Eating out

Ask for gluten free, then ask how the dish is cooked. A shared fryer, a shared grill, flour in the sauce and soy sauce in a marinade are the practical follow-ups. Yorkshire pudding, stuffing and gravy are wheat dishes unless the kitchen has a gluten free version.

Questions people ask

Is gluten free in the UK 20 ppm?

Yes. Assimilated Regulation (EU) No 828/2014 allows the words gluten free when the food, as sold, contains no more than 20 mg of gluten per kilogram, which is 20 ppm. That includes dishes in restaurants when the menu uses those words.

What does the Crossed Grain symbol add?

Coeliac UK licenses it for products at or under 20 mg/kg that have been checked against the AOECS standard. The legal statement is still the words gluten free, which many products use without the symbol. A licence code is printed with the mark, and UK codes start with UK.

What should I do with an NGCI menu?

NGCI means no gluten-containing ingredients. FSA guidance asks kitchens to reserve gluten free for food they control to 20 mg/kg, and to say when an NGCI list was not prepared to that standard. Ask which standard that menu is using.

Are barley and rye shown on the label?

Yes. Wheat, rye, barley, oats, spelt and khorasan wheat are emphasised in the ingredient list, usually in bold, when they are ingredients. Prepacked sandwiches made on site follow the same idea.
