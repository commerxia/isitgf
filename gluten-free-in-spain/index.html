Gluten free in Spain

Sin gluten on a Spanish label means 20 mg of gluten per kilogram or less. The Espiga Barrada is the crossed-grain mark you will see on licensed packs. The ingredient list uses trigo, cebada, centeno, avena and espelta. Save this for your next trip ✈️

Updated 18 November 2026

What sin gluten means

Spain applies Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 828/2014. Sin gluten may be used when the food, as sold, contains no more than 20 mg of gluten per kilogram (20 ppm).

Muy bajo en gluten means very low gluten: no more than 100 mg/kg, for food containing wheat, rye, barley, oats or their crosses that have been specially processed to reduce gluten. The 20 mg/kg statement is sin gluten.

Oats in a sin gluten food have to be specially produced to avoid contamination from wheat, rye and barley, and the oats stay at or under 20 mg/kg. Avena is still named in the ingredients.

When a menu describes a dish as sin gluten, that statement follows the same 20 mg/kg rule.

Sin gluten means 20 mg/kg or less. Muy bajo en gluten is the separate 100 mg/kg statement.

The Espiga Barrada

The Espiga Barrada is the AOECS Crossed Grain trademark. FACE, the Federación de Asociaciones de Celíacos de España, licenses it in Spain. A licensed product meets the AOECS standard of 20 mg/kg or less, with production checks. The symbol is printed with a licence code, and Spanish codes start with ES. Oat products show the word OATS with the symbol.

You will see the Espiga Barrada on licensed gluten-free packs in Spanish shops. Sin gluten remains the legal statement, including on foods that do not carry the symbol.

Words on the label

The cereal has to be named and emphasised, usually in bold. Trigo in the ingredients is wheat, even when the word gluten never appears.

Ingredients that often carry gluten

  • Trigo, espelta, kamut, sémola, cuscús and bulgur. Espelta is spelt and contains gluten.
  • Cebada and malta, including malt in drinks and breakfast cereals.
  • Centeno in bread and flour.
  • Avena, unless the pack says sin gluten.
  • Almidón de trigo on a food that is not sin gluten. A sin gluten food may use wheat starch when the finished food stays at or under 20 mg/kg.
  • Salsa de soja brewed with wheat.
  • Pan rallado, rebozado (batter) and croquetas bound with wheat flour, unless a sin gluten version is stated.
  • Cerveza, unless it is labeled sin gluten.
  • Seitán, which is wheat gluten. Some processed meats use wheat as a binder, and the ingredient list names trigo when it does.

Eating out

Bread is on the table, and a lot of starters are breaded or battered. Ask about the rebozado, the sauce and the fryer. A dish labeled sin gluten follows the 20 mg/kg rule, which includes how the kitchen controls cross-contact.

Questions people ask

What does sin gluten mean in Spain?

Under EU Regulation 828/2014, sin gluten means the food as sold contains no more than 20 mg of gluten per kilogram. Menus that use the same words are covered by that rule too.

What does "puede contener trazas de gluten" mean?

It is a voluntary precaution about possible cross-contact. Ingredients that are definitely in the food are listed with the cereal emphasised, such as trigo, cebada, centeno, avena or espelta.

What is the Espiga Barrada?

It is the Crossed Grain symbol. FACE licenses it in Spain for products at or under 20 mg/kg, with a licence code starting with ES. Sin gluten is the legal statement and also appears on products without the symbol.

Is espelta gluten free?

Espelta is spelt, a species of wheat, and it contains gluten. A pack is sin gluten when it carries that statement and meets the 20 mg/kg rule.
