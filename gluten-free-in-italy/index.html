Gluten free in Italy

Senza glutine on an Italian label means 20 mg of gluten per kilogram or less. The Spiga Barrata is the crossed-grain licence from AIC, and the ingredients speak in frumento, orzo, segale, avena and farro. Save this for your next trip ✈️

What senza glutine means

Italy applies Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 828/2014. Senza glutine may be used when the food, as sold, contains no more than 20 mg of gluten per kilogram (20 ppm).

Con contenuto di glutine molto basso means very low gluten: no more than 100 mg/kg, for food made with wheat, rye, barley, oats or their crosses specially processed to reduce gluten. The statement for the 20 mg/kg standard is senza glutine.

Oats in a senza glutine food have to be specially produced to avoid wheat, rye and barley, and the oats stay at or under 20 mg/kg. Avena is still named in the ingredients.

A restaurant dish described as senza glutine uses the same statement, so the 20 mg/kg rule includes that plate.

Senza glutine means 20 mg/kg or less. Con contenuto di glutine molto basso is the separate 100 mg/kg statement.

People with a coeliac diagnosis can receive gluten-free products through the Italian national health service. Those products are notified to the Ministry of Health. That supply route is separate from the senza glutine statement you read on any pack in the shop.

The Spiga Barrata

The Spiga Barrata is the AOECS Crossed Grain trademark. AIC, the Associazione Italiana Celiachia, licenses it in Italy. A licensed product meets the AOECS standard of 20 mg/kg or less, with production checks. The symbol carries a licence code, and Italian codes start with IT. Oat products show the word OATS with the symbol.

AIC also runs Alimentazione Fuori Casa, its programme for eating out. A listing there is a starting point for choosing a venue. Ask the kitchen about your order on the day. Senza glutine on the menu is the statement tied to the 20 mg/kg rule.

Words on the label

The cereal is named and emphasised, usually in bold. Frumento or grano in the list is wheat.

Ingredients that often carry gluten

  • Frumento, grano, farro, kamut, semola and couscous. Farro is wheat. Ordinary farro soup and farro bread are wheat foods.
  • Orzo and malto, including malt in drinks and packaged sweets.
  • Segale in bread and flour.
  • Avena, unless the pack says senza glutine.
  • Amido di frumento on a food that is not senza glutine. A senza glutine food may list wheat starch when the finished food stays at or under 20 mg/kg.
  • Salsa di soia brewed with wheat.
  • Pangrattato and anything impanato. Fresh pasta, pizza dough and pane are wheat unless a senza glutine version is offered.
  • Birra, unless it is labeled senza glutine.
  • Seitan, which is wheat gluten.

Eating out

Pasta, pizza, bread and beer are the everyday wheat and barley foods. Ask which dishes are senza glutine, then ask where they are prepared. In a pizzeria, flour from the wheat dough travels easily, so ask whether the gluten-free pizza is assembled away from it. The same question applies to pasta water.

Questions people ask

What does senza glutine mean in Italy?

Under EU Regulation 828/2014, senza glutine means the food as sold contains no more than 20 mg of gluten per kilogram. A restaurant dish that uses the same words follows that rule.

What does "può contenere tracce di glutine" mean?

It is a voluntary precaution about possible cross-contact with gluten. Cereals in the recipe are named in the ingredient list, such as frumento, orzo, segale, avena or farro.

What is the Spiga Barrata?

It is the Crossed Grain symbol, licensed in Italy by AIC for products at or under 20 mg/kg. The licence code starts with IT. Senza glutine is the legal statement and also appears on products without the symbol.

Is farro gluten free?

Farro is hulled wheat (emmer, spelt or einkorn) and it contains gluten. A pack is senza glutine when it carries that statement and meets the 20 mg/kg rule.
