Gluten free in Argentina?
You're gonna LOVE it 🇦🇷🥩

Argentina has its own word for gluten free: sin TACC. You'll see it on menus, in kioscos and on packages. Here's what it means, what to eat, and a card in Spanish to show the kitchen. Save this for your next trip ✈️

Sin TACC: the four letters to know

TACC stands for Trigo, Avena, Cebada, Centeno (wheat, oats, barley, rye). Packaged foods approved as gluten free carry the round "Sin TACC" logo with a crossed-out wheat ear. Many cafés and bakeries advertise sin TACC options, and some are fully gluten free.

Ask "¿es sin TACC?" anywhere. People will know exactly what you mean.

Favourites to look for

  • Asado: grilled beef at a parrilla, with provoleta (grilled cheese) and salads. Ask about sausages and sauces.
  • Chimichurri: herbs, garlic, vinegar and oil. Usually naturally gluten free.
  • Locro and humita: corn-based stews from the north, great in winter. Ask about thickeners.
  • Dulce de leche: look for the Sin TACC logo on the jar.
  • Sin TACC alfajores and empanadas: dedicated versions are easy to find in big cities.

Worth asking about

  • Milanesas: breaded with wheat breadcrumbs unless they say sin TACC.
  • Regular empanadas, medialunas and facturas: wheat dough.
  • Alfajores de maicena: despite the cornstarch name, most recipes also use wheat flour.
  • Chorizo and morcilla: some sausages contain fillers. Ask, or check for Sin TACC.
  • Shared grills and fryers: papas fritas often share oil with milanesas.
  • Beer (cerveza): made from barley.

Recipes vary by cook and region, so ask every time and check labels. This guide helps you plan; it doesn't replace your own judgement or medical advice.

Useful phrases
